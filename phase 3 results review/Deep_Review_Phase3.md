# 1. Completeness & Blind Spots

The pipeline is unusually complete for this stage: full 21×10 grid, both latency arms, mechanism instrumentation, minority-survival tracking, degenerate-dose exclusion from slope fits, time-averaged prequential metrics as primary, HL estimators + rank-biserial + global Holm, oracle provenance qualified, and a withdrawn causal claim replaced by an identified 2×2. That last item — publicly retracting your own confounded decomposition and redesigning it — is the strongest signal of research maturity in the repo. Four genuine blind spots remain, all in Phase 3's causal chain, all cheap to close:

**1.1 The repair experiment is not error-matched — and the confound is 8:1.** FP-repair removes 6,565 wrong labels; FN-repair adds 837 right ones (verified). You've flagged this caveat, but a flag is not a control. As it stands, "FP-repair wins" conflates *which error type is costlier per unit* with *which error type there is more of*. Add a **matched-mass condition**: remove a random 837 of the FPs (seed-varied) and compare against restoring all 837 FNs. Better still, run **repair-fraction curves** (repair 25/50/75/100% of each error type): the two slopes are the *marginal value per corrected label* of each error type — a far stronger, dose-controlled version of the same claim, and it reuses all existing machinery.

**1.2 FN-repair tests "FNs delivered at realistic delays," not "FNs."** Restored FNs get imputed fix_ts from the empirical pool (median 113d; >50% arrive post-window as wrong-clean-then-corrected). Your own synthesis says of the analogous oracle imputation: *handing labels at realistic delays is close to a no-op.* By the same logic, FN-repair's null result cannot distinguish **"the missing labels don't matter"** from **"the missing labels can't be delivered in time to matter."** Add a **delivery-ceiling condition**: restore FNs with immediate arrival (fix_ts = author_ts + ε, or at t+W as a softer ceiling). If the ceiling condition also recovers nothing, the content claim stands; if it recovers substantially, your conclusion becomes the sharper and more interesting *"recall is unmonetizable under realistic latency"* — a scheduling result, not a content result.

**1.3 There is no true no-latency arm, so the compression refutation overreaches.** Your "uniform" arm delivers every label at t+90d — both arms are heavily delayed; they differ only in arrival *schedule*. "Real-arm and uniform-arm slopes are within noise" therefore refutes *schedule sensitivity*, not *latency masking*. To test masking as Phase 2 framed it, you need an **immediate-label arm** (labels at t+ε). Prediction worth registering: the no-latency arm will show steeper, better-separated profile slopes. One extra `latency_arms` entry; the runner already supports it structurally.

**1.4 "FN-repair recovers nothing" is currently absence-of-evidence.** The CI [−0.0093, +0.0080] is tight enough to upgrade it: run a **TOST equivalence test** with a pre-declared margin (±0.02 MCC is defensible — half the FP-repair effect). Passing TOST converts "no significant difference" into the affirmative "equivalent to no repair within ±0.02," which is the claim your conclusion actually needs. Examiners who know the difference will notice you made it.

Minor completeness items: the synthesis (line ~828) still says Phase 3 is "awaiting compute" — stale against committed results; the Phase 4 runner's docstring still carries the retired FN framing (see §4); `support_codebase/` still lingers. Standard-metric coverage is otherwise complete — MCC/G-mean/κ, per-class recalls implicit in G-mean, effect sizes throughout. One optional addition an examiner may ask for: **PR-AUC or cost-effort curves (e.g., Recall@20%-effort)** for the headline configurations, since JIT-SDP's practical framing is effort-aware inspection; a single appendix figure suffices.

# 2. Results Analysis & Interpretation

**Independently verified:** FP-repair beats BSZZ by +0.0385 mean / +0.0402 HL (17/21 projects, p=0.0002, global Holm 0.0019, rank-biserial 0.84); FN-repair −0.0012 HL, CI [−0.0093, +0.0080], 8/21; oracle beats BSZZ +0.0258 (16/21, p=0.010); **FP-repaired BSZZ numerically beats the oracle itself** (+0.0127, 14/21, p=0.089). Slopes: fn_heavy degrades fastest in dose-response (−0.036/10% vs −0.020 fp_heavy, non-degenerate scope); arms statistically indistinguishable.

**What it means — you now have a complete, mechanism-level account. Write it exactly like this:**

The mechanism traces contain the reconciliation you committed but haven't fully surfaced. Mean Poisson rate λ received by positive-labeled arrivals: under fn_heavy, λ runs 20–35 (rising as positives get scarcer); under fp_heavy/symmetric, 9–25. That is direct evidence that **ORB's boost is a built-in FN compensator**: when positive labels are removed, the boost amplifies the survivors harder, partially replacing the lost mass. But the boost has **no mechanism to discount a wrong positive** — a false positive receives the same amplification as a true one. Hence the asymmetry: real BSZZ's 837 FNs are (a) few, (b) mostly late-delivered anyway, and (c) already compensated by the boost — restoring them adds ~nothing. Its 6,565 FPs are (a) 8× more numerous and (b) *actively amplified* — removing them recovers +0.04. The dose-response ordering (fn_heavy steepest) doesn't contradict this: at matched *total-commit* dose, the FN-heavy profile strips a huge fraction of the 8.5% minority, entering the annihilation regime your slope-exclusion correctly quarantines; per-class dose accounting (report ρ₁ as a fraction of positives alongside) dissolves the apparent tension. Your commit message calls the denominator "the substantive point" — correct, and the λ traces are its physiological proof.

**The unifying quantity across Phases 2–3 is *deliverable precision*.** Phase 2: oracle significantly beats every precise-but-blind variant yet ties recall-heavy BSZZ. Phase 3: fully-delivered, precision-repaired BSZZ edges the 68%-delivered perfect oracle. One sentence carries the thesis: *what an online learner needs is not correct labels in the abstract, but correct labels that arrive — and among labels that arrive, false positives are the error the learner cannot defend against, because its own imbalance mechanism amplifies them.* Every result in the repo is a corollary of that sentence — including the retirement of FN-starvation.

**Statistical vs practical significance, candidly:** the FP-repair effect is statistically bulletproof (rank-biserial 0.84 means near-uniform direction across projects). Practically, +0.04 on a ~0.06 baseline is a ~70% relative gain — meaningful for ranking-based effort allocation — but the absolute ceiling remains low: even perfect-precision labels leave ORB near MCC 0.10 on this benchmark. Do not let the thesis imply Phase 4 will make JIT-SDP *good*; it can make it *less self-sabotaging*. That honesty is also your shield: the low ceiling is itself a headline finding of Phase 2.

**One caution on the FP-repaired>oracle comparison:** it uses oracle knowledge to edit BSZZ, so it is an *oracle-assisted upper bound*, not an achievable labeler. Frame it as "the value of precision, holding deliverability fixed," never as a practical recommendation. Phase 4's job is to approximate that repair *without* oracle access — which is now a beautifully crisp problem statement.

# 3. Better Solutions & Optimizations

**What I would not change:** the numpy ORB (validated against Cabral, fast, instrumented — switching to `river` now buys nothing and costs revalidation); the statistics stack (HL/rank-biserial/Holm/paired design is above field standard); the CI-gated reproducibility posture; time-averaged prequential as primary (correct: terminal MCC reflects ~100 commits under fading 0.99, and your own repair stats show terminal-metric tests losing all significance — the *choice* of mcc_avg is what made Phase 3 conclusive, document that explicitly).

**What has a strictly better alternative:**

1. **Binary repair → repair-fraction curves** (§1.1). Same compute order, converts a 2-condition contrast into marginal-value-per-label slopes, and error-matching falls out for free at the intersection point.
2. **Phase 4's headline model is now mechanism-misaligned.** The rescue path injects provisional positives — which is FN-repair by another name, and FN-repair just measured ≈0; worse, every *false* rescue manufactures exactly the error type Phase 3 proved costly. The mechanism-aligned primary is **FP-suppression**: your damp path, or — likely stronger and simpler — **Backup A filtering** (train-time discard/down-weight of positive labels the ensemble confidently contradicts). Rationale: damp still lets a suspicious positive in at OOB weight; filtering removes it, which is what the repair experiment says pays. Concretely, an `fp_filter` mode: positive-labeled arrival with ensemble p₁ below a running low-quantile threshold → weight ε. Two hyperparameters, no calibration dependence, directly mimics FP-repair without oracle access.
3. **Keep rescue in the grid as a falsifiable mechanism probe, not a candidate.** Register the prediction *rescue ≤ 0* — if it lands, your Phase 3 mechanism story is confirmed by an independent intervention; if rescue helps, the story needs revision. Either way it earns its compute.
4. **Loss correction:** with FP the dominant delivered-label harm, the correction that matters is down-weighting *positives* by measured precision (per variant, from Phase 1) — a one-line reweighting (w₊ = precision̂) that is more stable than the capped Natarajan form and directly interpretable. Add as one ablation arm.
5. Efficiency nits: score self/oracle conventions from one prediction stream (halves ORB cost on any rerun); the O(n²) Cliff's delta is fine at n=21 but you now use rank-biserial anyway — retire the old function to avoid dual effect-size conventions in the text.
